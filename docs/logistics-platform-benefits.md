# How TradeLoop Improves the Business

**For:** the owner and business partners of Lucena Fresh Trading & Logistics
**About:** what the Phase 1 logistics platform changes in daily operations, and where the money and time come from

> All figures in this document come from the **demo dataset**: fictional customers, demo freight rates and simulated trips. They show how the platform measures the business. They are not forecasts. Replace them with your own numbers during the first month of use (see [Measuring the impact](#8-measuring-the-impact)).

---

## 1. Summary

Today, the business runs on Facebook Messenger, phone calls, a notebook and the owner's memory. It works, but only while the owner is watching. It breaks down when:

- volume grows
- a key person is away
- a customer disputes a delivery

TradeLoop puts every shipment into one connected record. The record starts with the first inquiry. It ends when the trip's profitability is known.

```
Lead → Quote → Logistics Job → Loads → Dispatch → Trip & Stops → Delivery & POD
     → Backhaul → Trip Costs → Invoice → Collection → Trip Profitability
```

The five outcomes that matter most:

1. **Fuller trucks on both legs.** Unused return capacity is visible on every trip while the truck is still in Manila. There is still time to fill it.
2. **Every delivery billed and every peso chased.** POD creates the invoice automatically. Overdue balances age in plain view.
3. **Know which trips make money.** Each trip shows revenue, costs and contribution, by truck, lane and customer.
4. **Fewer surprises on the road.** Before a truck is dispatched, the platform flags:
   - overloads
   - drivers on leave
   - maintenance that is due
   - expired permits
5. **The business keeps running without the owner on the phone.** Staff work from the same records. The owner reviews in minutes instead of making calls.

## 3. How the platform works

Each step is done by the person who already does that job. The record they create is picked up by the next person, with nothing retyped.

| Step | Who | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lead** | Sales | Every inquiry is logged with cargo, lane and expected volume. The pipeline shows which lanes are worth chasing. |
| **Quote** | Sales | Rates come from one rate card. An accepted quote becomes the job with one click. |
| **Logistics job** | Sales | One record per shipment, whatever the channel: Messenger, phone, Facebook, walk-in or portal. |
| **Loads** | Warehouse | Cargo is broken into boxes and sacks with gross weight. It is checked against the van's configured payload. |
| **Dispatch** | Dispatcher | Unassigned jobs sit next to today's trips and their remaining capacity. Warnings appear before assignment. |
| **Trip & stops** | Dispatcher, driver | Stops are planned automatically. The driver marks arrivals from a phone, so progress is visible without calls. |
| **Delivery & POD** | Driver | Captured at the drop: receiver, DR number, signature, photos, and short or damaged kilos. |
| **Backhaul** | Dispatcher, procurement | Return capacity is shown per trip. Paid return cargo and company-owned produce fill it. |
| **Trip costs** | Driver, accounting | Diesel, tolls, allowances and fees are logged against the trip. Diesel is estimated until the fill-up is recorded. |
| **Invoice** | Automatic | Created when POD is captured. Due date follows the customer's payment terms. |
| **Collection** | Accounting | Payments are recorded with OR numbers: cash, COD, GCash, Maya, bank transfer or check. Balances age automatically. |
| **Trip profitability** | Owner | Revenue minus trip costs gives each trip's contribution. It rolls up by truck, lane, customer and week. |

To see the loop with live demo records, open **How It Works** (`/workflow`) in the app. It follows four real demo shipments step by step:

- a Messenger booking
- a new customer won from a Facebook lead
- a backhaul job
- a fully paid job

## 4. Benefits by area

### 4.1 Bookings and customers

- **One inbox for all channels.** Staff still chat on Messenger and take calls. Each confirmed booking becomes a logistics job, so the customer does not have to change how they order.
- **Consistent pricing.** Quotes use one rate card (lane, weight, truck requirement) plus listed additional charges, so two staff members no longer quote two prices.
- **Customer history in one place.** Each customer profile shows:
  - jobs and kilos shipped
  - lifetime freight
  - outstanding and overdue balances
  - payment terms and credit limit
- **Leads that don't go cold.** The pipeline runs New → Contacted → Quoted → Sample → Negotiating → Won or Lost. Next steps and expected monthly value are recorded. Winning a lead converts it to a customer.

### 4.2 Dispatch and truck utilization

- **Capacity before commitment.** Every trip shows outbound and return kilos against the configured payload of 8,500 kg per van. The dispatcher sees what still fits before saying yes to a customer.
- **Problems caught at the desk, not on the road.** Assignment warns about:
  - over-capacity
  - drivers on leave or rest day
  - trucks due for maintenance
  - expired vehicle documents
  - double-booked trucks
- **Automatic stop planning.** Adding or moving a job re-plans the stop order and estimated arrivals. Late-risk drops are flagged against each customer's required time.

### 4.3 Trips and deliveries

- **Progress without phone calls.** The driver's phone view shows stops, consignee contacts, cargo and instructions. Marking arrival and delivery updates the trip for the dispatcher and the owner at once.
- **POD the moment cargo is received.** The receiver's name, DR number, signature, photos, and short or damaged kilos are recorded at the drop. Disputes are settled with evidence, not memory.
- **Issues logged on the spot.** Late arrival, rejected cargo, road problems and consignee no-shows are recorded against the delivery. They feed the on-time and damage reports.

### 4.4 Backhaul: the biggest lever

A trip is not finished when the last seafood drop is made. The truck still has to drive 150–180 km back to Lucena. Diesel, tolls and the crew's time are spent whether the van is full or empty.

The platform treats the return leg as capacity to sell:

- **Return utilization is shown on every trip**, next to outbound utilization.
- **Unused return kilos are flagged while the truck is still out.** The dispatcher can still add return cargo during the Dispatched and In Transit stages.
- **Three ways to fill it**, each tracked separately:
  - paid backhaul jobs
  - third-party cargo
  - company-owned produce, such as onion, garlic and ginger bought for resale
- **Matching is explainable.** Suggestions are based on:
  - route overlap
  - remaining capacity
  - pickup timing

  There is no hidden "AI". The dispatcher can see why each suggestion was made.
- **Loaded vs. empty kilometers** are tracked, so the owner can see how much of the fleet's driving earns money.

### 4.5 Fleet upkeep and compliance

- **Maintenance by odometer and date.** Each truck shows:
  - next PMS due (in km)
  - overdue work
  - upcoming service
  - full maintenance history with costs
- **Documents that don't lapse silently.** OR/CR, insurance, LTFRB and fish-port permits are tracked, along with driver licenses and clearances. Each is marked Valid, Expiring Soon (≤30 days) or Expired.
- **Fuel efficiency per fill-up.** Full-to-full fuel logs show km per liter for each truck. A sudden drop points to a mechanical issue, a route problem or a fuel leak.

### 4.6 Trip costs and profitability

- **Every expense tied to a trip.** Categories include diesel, tolls, driver and helper allowances, meals, parking, loading and unloading fees, port fees and repairs. Cash advances can be reconciled trip by trip.
- **Contribution per trip.** It is calculated as freight revenue plus additional charges, minus trip expenses. Until the diesel fill-up is logged, estimated diesel is included.

  This is **contribution**, not net income. Truck amortization, insurance, salaries and overhead sit outside the trip. The platform labels it that way on purpose.
- **Cost per kilometer**, by truck and lane. It is the basis for pricing a new lane or renegotiating an old one.

### 4.7 Billing and collections

- **No delivered job goes unbilled.** The freight invoice is created from the job's charges when POD is captured. It is dated on the delivery and due according to the customer's terms.
- **Every payment traceable.** Payments are recorded against the invoice with an OR number and method: cash, COD, GCash, Maya, bank transfer or check. Partial payments are supported.
- **Aging that the owner sees daily.** Balances are grouped into Current, 1–7, 8–30, 31–60 and 60+ days. The largest overdue accounts appear on the Command Center.
- **Better credit decisions.** Credit terms and limits can be set from each customer's actual payment history, not from how friendly the last conversation was.

### 4.8 Owner visibility

The Command Center is built so the owner can understand the day in under a minute. It shows:

- trucks on the road and their progress
- deliveries done and at risk
- jobs awaiting dispatch
- today's freight revenue
- overdue accounts
- fleet alerts

The Backhaul screen shows each trip's unused return capacity. Reports roll everything up across 15 views, including:

- trip profitability
- revenue by trip, route and customer
- truck and backhaul utilization, and empty capacity
- fuel and maintenance cost
- delivery performance
- receivables aging
- lead conversion

## 5. The numbers: a demo illustration

The demo dataset simulates about two months of operations with two 10-wheeler closed vans. Figures below are **demo values**.

### What the platform measures

| Measure (completed demo trips) | Demo value |
| --- | --- |
| Average outbound utilization | 67% of configured payload |
| Average return utilization | 36% of configured payload |
| Average revenue per trip | ₱69,064 |
| Average recorded trip expenses | ₱16,650 |
| Average trip contribution | ₱52,414 |
| Freight receivables outstanding | ₱498,775 |
| — of which overdue | ₱164,100 (33%) |
| — 31 days or more overdue | ₱35,100 |

### Where improvement comes from

**1. Return capacity.** At 36% return utilization, an 8,500 kg van comes home with about **5,400 kg of unused space**.

Worked example, using the demo backhaul rate of ₱5/kg:

- Filling just 1,500 kg more per return leg adds about **₱7,500 per trip**.
- The diesel, toll and crew cost of that leg is already paid, so almost all of it is contribution.
- At 20 return legs a month, that is roughly **₱150,000 a month** of additional contribution.

Plug in your own rate and volume. The platform shows the real gap on every trip.

**2. Collections.** A third of the demo receivables are past due. Every peso collected earlier is working capital that doesn't need to be borrowed.

The platform does not collect money by itself. What it does:

- makes overdue balances impossible to overlook
- gives accounting a prioritized list
- gives the owner the payment history to tighten terms for slow payers

**3. Unbilled and disputed deliveries.** With paper DRs:

- some deliveries are billed late
- some are billed wrong
- a few are never billed at all
- short or damaged claims are settled by argument

When POD creates the invoice, the leak is closed. Photos and signatures settle most disputes.

**4. Fuel and maintenance.** A truck stopped by a lapsed permit, or broken down from a missed PMS, loses a full trip's contribution. In the demo, that is about ₱52,000, before repair costs and the customer relationship.

Fuel efficiency tracking catches a slow decline before it becomes a large monthly cost.

**5. Staff time.** Calls to find a truck, retyping bookings, reconciling receipts and building reports by hand all shrink. That time goes back to selling and to finding backhaul cargo.

## 7. Adoption plan

The platform is designed to fit how the business already works. Customers keep ordering on Messenger and by phone. The customer portal is an extra channel, not a requirement.

| Phase | Weeks | What starts | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Record | 1–2 | Every confirmed booking is entered as a logistics job. Trips and loads are built on the dispatch board. | Sales, dispatcher |
| 2. Deliver | 3–4 | Drivers use the phone view to mark arrivals and capture POD. Trip expenses are logged per trip. | Drivers, accounting |
| 3. Bill & collect | 5–6 | Invoices come from POD, and payments are recorded with OR numbers. Aging is reviewed weekly. | Accounting, owner |
| 4. Optimize | 7+ | Weekly backhaul review, lane pricing from cost per km, and credit terms from payment history. | Owner, dispatcher |

Practical tips:

- **Start with one truck** for the first two weeks. Then add the second.
- **Enter opening balances** for customers who already owe money, so aging is right from day one.
- **Keep the rate card current.** Quotes are only as consistent as the rates behind them.
- **Make POD non-negotiable.** Billing, disputes and reporting all depend on it.

## 9. What Phase 1 does not do (yet)

To stay honest about scope:

- **No live GPS.** Truck location comes from the driver's latest stop update.
- **No automatic route optimization.** Stop order is planned by simple rules. The dispatcher can still adjust it.
- **No AI forecasting or price intelligence.** Backhaul suggestions are rule-based and explained.
- **No accounting-system or e-wallet integration.** Payments are recorded manually with their reference numbers.
- **The trading module** (product sales, stock, purchasing) is available as a Phase 2 preview. It does not feed trips or cargo.
- **This version is a frontend prototype with demo data.** A production rollout needs:
  - a backend
  - user accounts and permissions
  - data backup

These appear in the app under **Future Modules**. They would be considered once Phase 1 is part of daily operations.
